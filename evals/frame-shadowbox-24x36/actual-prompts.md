## COMMON DESIGN PROMPT — identical for both candidates:

Design a modular, store-bought-looking wall-hung shadow-box frame for geometric art made from colored paper and 3D shapes on a mat board. The artwork/display size is 24 x 36 INCHES (609.6 x 914.4 mm), not the frame outside size. The art protrudes at most 0.75 inch (19.05 mm) from the mat surface. Provide at least 1 inch (25.4 mm) of USABLE depth from that surface to the inside of the transparent front, leaving at least 6.35 mm nominal clearance above the art; account for stack-up, tolerances and panel bow. Explicitly dimension the visible display, artwork/mat seat, any lip overlap, outer frame, glazing and backing cut sizes. Do not silently crop the specified display area. Portrait orientation and indoor use are assumptions; state implications for landscape mounting.

Printer: Bambu Lab P2S, build volume 256 x 256 x 256 mm; initially assume 0.4 mm nozzle. The printer has not arrived. Every individual printable part, including joints/tabs, must fit in its stated print orientation with practical room for brim/margins. The whole frame cannot be printed as one piece. An oversized assembly visualization is not a printable deliverable. Plan modular rails/corners, concealed alignment and fastening, a practical assembly sequence, seam placement, accumulated tolerance/racking and finishing. Explain how to achieve a near-seamless store-bought appearance without falsely promising invisible raw-print seams. Include a small mating/joint test coupon where useful.

The user permits either primarily printed structure with ordinary metal fasteners/hardware OR printed sections with hidden wood/aluminum reinforcement. Choose and justify. Provide a credible load path from glazing/frame through joints or reinforcement to purchased hanging hardware and the wall. Do not rely on untested printed hanging tabs, adhesive alone, paper mat or thin nonstructural backing to support a large heavy assembly. Wall type/studs and finished mass are unknown: give conditional mounting instructions, measurement/check steps, and no fabricated load rating or certification.

Choose glass, acrylic or another suitable transparent front. Justify weight, breakage/clarity/scratch tradeoffs. Explain insertion AND later removal, retaining lip/rabbet, spacer/backing/retainer stack, cut size, nominal thickness, material-specific movement clearance and support. Prefer serviceable construction. Source numeric recommendations rather than treating generic search summaries as engineering evidence.

Research real web references and give at least three aesthetic options: matte-black gallery, warm wood-look, and stepped white. Fully engineer and model ONE recommended option; label other options as concepts unless actually generated. Starting references available equally to both candidates: https://frameusa.com/pages/24x36-shadow-box ; https://www.custompictureframes.com/shadow-box-frames/sizes/24x36 ; https://bambulab.com/en/p2s/specs ; https://www.acrylite.co/products/brands/acrylite-gallery/framing-grade ; https://www.acrylite.co/applications/framing-and-picture-glazing . Verify any specifics you rely on; normal public research and CAD documentation are allowed. Do not copy proprietary model assets or unlicensed imagery.

Deliver: editable CAD/source and reproducible export commands; STL per unique printable part with quantities/orientations; actual geometry-derived assembly/section/joint views, not unrelated AI images; three aesthetic options with cited references; complete BOM and cut list; sequential printing, fit-checking, finishing, glazing, assembly and hanging instructions; measured geometry/dimension/printer-fit checks with assumptions and unresolved physical risks. STEP is welcome if supported, but editable OpenSCAD is also valid. Separate measured values, estimates and manufacturer ratings. State tool/dependency versions and source/output hashes where practical. No physical-print/slicer claims unless actually performed. No printer connection, auto-print, purchases, paid generation APIs, or uploading user data. Preserve intermediate failures honestly. Do not claim mechanical function is proven by valid meshes.
